utterpy 0.0.7 carries utter 0.0.7, which adds a merge margin: a setting that keeps, among the readings, those the search overwrites while they are close to the path that wins. It is off by default, and off, the output is the same as 0.0.6. This release also adds Python 3.15, and is the second on PyPI.

## Added

`rec.SetMergeMargin(nats)` sets the margin, in nats. Where two paths with different words meet in the search, the decoder keeps the cheaper and the other's history is gone, even when it was a fraction of a nat behind. With a margin, a loser within it of the winner is kept and listed among `partial_alternatives` and the final's `alternatives`, with confidence, relation, lead_delta and word entries like any other reading. Rank 0, the endpoint rules, the host's bound and its veto, and every key outside the readings are unchanged. `SetMergeMargin(0)` or `SetMergeMargin(None)` removes it, as with `SetEndpointFloorMargin`.

The margin offers what was close, not what was right: over the Speech Commands test clips, a kept reading closer than the right word sometimes takes its place among the first few alternatives. A margin of 3 costs about 13 µs per 40 ms block. [TD-18](https://github.com/pyscape/utter/blob/main/docs/td/0018-a-merge-keeps-the-readings-it-overwrites-within-a-margin.md) says why, and utter's [release notes](https://github.com/pyscape/utter/blob/main/docs/release-notes/v0.0.7.md) have the measurements.

Python 3.15 is tested in CI and listed in the classifiers. The wheels were already installable on it through the stable ABI.

## Install

```bash
pip install utterpy==0.0.7
```

Wheels for Linux x86_64 and aarch64 (manylinux 2_28 and musllinux 1.2), Windows x64 and arm64, and macOS x86_64 and arm64, Python 3.9 and later through the stable ABI. Every wheel carries a build-provenance attestation, on PyPI and here, and the release attaches its Sigstore bundle:

```bash
gh attestation verify utterpy-0.0.7-cp39-abi3-manylinux_2_28_x86_64.whl --bundle utterpy-v0.0.7.sigstore.json --owner pyscape
```
